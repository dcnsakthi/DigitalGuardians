🔒 Privacy Protection Guide

Learn how to protect your personal information and maintain your privacy online.

Understanding Digital Privacy

Digital privacy is about controlling what information you share online and who can access it. Every time you use the internet, you leave behind data about yourself—your location, browsing habits, purchases, and more.

💡 Key Principle: Only share information that you're comfortable being public forever. Once something is online, it's very difficult to completely remove it.

Personal Information to Protect

  • Full name and birthday - Can be used for identity theft
  • Home address - Physical safety risk
  • Phone number - Can lead to unwanted contact
  • Email address - Use different emails for different purposes
  • Financial information - Never share card numbers or bank details
  • School name - Can help predators locate you
  • Photos with location data - Can reveal where you live or spend time
  • Daily routines - Don't post your schedule online

Privacy Settings Checklist

Social Media

  • Set your profile to private
  • Review who can see your posts
  • Limit who can tag you in photos
  • Turn off location sharing
  • Review friend/follower lists regularly
  • Disable third-party app access

Mobile Devices

  • Review app permissions regularly
  • Turn off location services when not needed
  • Disable ad tracking
  • Use app-specific passwords when available
  • Enable "Find My Device" features

Web Browsers

  • Use privacy-focused browsers or extensions
  • Clear cookies and cache regularly
  • Use "Do Not Track" settings
  • Enable pop-up blockers
  • Consider using a VPN for sensitive activities

Smart Privacy Habits

1. Think Before You Post

Ask yourself: Would I be comfortable with this being seen by my family, teachers, or future employers?

2. Use Strong, Unique Passwords

Create different passwords for different accounts. Use a mix of uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols.

3. Enable Two-Factor Authentication

This adds an extra layer of security by requiring a code from your phone in addition to your password.

4. Be Cautious with Public Wi-Fi

Avoid accessing sensitive information on public networks. Use a VPN if you must use public Wi-Fi.

5. Review Your Digital Footprint

Regularly search for yourself online to see what information is publicly available.

What to Do If Your Privacy Is Compromised

  1. Document everything - Take screenshots of the privacy breach
  2. Report it - Contact the platform or website immediately
  3. Change passwords - Update passwords for affected accounts
  4. Tell a trusted adult - Don't try to handle it alone
  5. Consider legal action - For serious breaches, consult with authorities

Resources for Parents

As a parent, you can help protect your child's privacy by:

  • Having open conversations about what they share online
  • Setting up parental controls on devices
  • Reviewing privacy settings together
  • Being a good role model with your own privacy practices
  • Teaching them to come to you with concerns
⚠️ Remember: Privacy isn't about having something to hide—it's about having control over your personal information.
