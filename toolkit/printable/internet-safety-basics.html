Introduction

Welcome to the Internet Safety Basics guide! This comprehensive resource is designed to help families of all ages understand and navigate the digital world safely. Whether you're a parent, child, or educator, this guide provides essential information to protect yourself and your loved ones online.

📚 What You'll Learn

  • Understanding common online risks and how to avoid them
  • Creating and managing strong passwords
  • Configuring privacy settings on devices and platforms
  • Practicing safe communication online
  • Recognizing and responding to online threats

Understanding Online Risks

Common Digital Threats

The internet offers incredible opportunities for learning, connection, and entertainment, but it also presents risks that everyone should understand:

Threat Description Who's at Risk
Cyberbullying Harassment, threats, or humiliation through digital platforms Children & Teens
Online Predators Adults who build relationships with children for harmful purposes Children & Teens
Phishing Scams Fraudulent attempts to obtain sensitive information All Ages
Malware Malicious software that can damage devices or steal data All Ages
Identity Theft Unauthorized use of personal information Teens & Adults
Privacy Violations Unauthorized collection or sharing of personal data All Ages

⚠️ Remember

Not all online threats are obvious. Many dangers disguise themselves as friendly interactions, helpful messages, or exciting opportunities. Always maintain a healthy level of skepticism online.

Creating Strong Passwords

Password Best Practices

Strong passwords are your first line of defense against unauthorized access to your accounts. Follow these guidelines to create secure passwords:

The Anatomy of a Strong Password

  • Length: At least 12-16 characters long
  • Complexity: Mix of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols
  • Unpredictability: Avoid dictionary words, personal information, or common patterns
  • Uniqueness: Use different passwords for different accounts

Password Examples

Weak ❌ Strong ✅ Why It Works
password123 Tr0p!c@l$unset#2025 Long, complex, unpredictable
johnsmith B1ueM0unta!n&Sk¥ No personal information, varied characters
12345678 P!zz@L0vers#7Cats Memorable phrase with substitutions

💡 Pro Tip: The Passphrase Method

Create a memorable passphrase using random words combined with numbers and symbols:

Example: "Correct-Horse-Battery-Staple-2025!"

This is easy to remember but extremely difficult to crack.

Password Management

  • Use a Password Manager: Tools like LastPass, 1Password, or Bitwarden can securely store and generate passwords
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA): Adds an extra layer of security beyond passwords
  • Change Passwords Regularly: Update important passwords every 3-6 months
  • Never Share Passwords: Exception: parents may need access to children's accounts
  • Avoid Writing Passwords Down: Unless stored in a secure, locked location

Privacy Settings Overview

Device Privacy Settings

Every device you use should be properly configured to protect your privacy:

Essential Privacy Configurations

🖥️ Computers (Windows/Mac)

  • Create separate user accounts for each family member
  • Enable automatic updates for operating system and security software
  • Turn on firewall protection
  • Disable location tracking when not needed
  • Review app permissions regularly
  • Enable screen lock with password/PIN after inactivity

📱 Mobile Devices (iOS/Android)

  • Use Face ID, fingerprint, or strong PIN to unlock device
  • Review and limit app permissions (camera, microphone, location)
  • Disable app tracking and ad personalization
  • Turn off Bluetooth and WiFi when not in use
  • Only download apps from official app stores
  • Keep operating system and apps updated

Online Account Privacy

Social Media Privacy Checklist

  • Set profile to private (friends/followers only)
  • Review who can see your posts and personal information
  • Disable location tagging in photos
  • Turn off facial recognition features
  • Review and remove old posts that reveal personal information
  • Limit who can tag you in photos or posts
  • Regularly review and update friend/follower lists

🔍 Privacy Check Schedule

Set a reminder to review your privacy settings every 3 months. Platforms frequently update their settings, and new features may expose more information than intended.

Safe Communication Practices

General Communication Rules

Think Before You Share

Before posting, sending, or sharing anything online, ask yourself:

  1. Would I be comfortable with everyone seeing this? (including parents, teachers, future employers)
  2. Could this information be used to identify or locate me?
  3. Could this hurt someone's feelings or reputation?
  4. Am I sharing someone else's personal information?
  5. Could I regret posting this in the future?

⚠️ The Permanence Principle

Once something is posted online, it can never be truly deleted. Screenshots, archives, and shares can preserve content forever. Always assume that anything you post will be permanent.

Information You Should NEVER Share Online

  • Full name and date of birth
  • Home address, school name, or workplace address
  • Phone number or email address (in public posts)
  • Social Security number or financial information
  • Passwords or login credentials
  • Travel plans (especially while away from home)
  • Real-time location information
  • Photos that reveal your location or daily routines
  • Personal conflicts or family issues

Communicating with Others

For Children & Teens

  • Talk to trusted adults about people you meet online
  • Never meet online friends in person without parent permission and supervision
  • Be cautious of compliments or gifts from people you don't know well
  • Block and report anyone who makes you uncomfortable
  • Don't accept friend requests from people you don't know

For Parents

  • Establish open communication with your children about their online activities
  • Know your children's online friends just as you would their in-person friends
  • Create family rules about online communication together
  • Monitor without invading privacy - build trust while maintaining oversight

💡 The Stranger Danger Rule Still Applies

If you wouldn't share information with a stranger on the street, don't share it with a stranger online. The internet doesn't change the basic safety rules we teach children about stranger danger.

Recognizing Online Threats

Warning Signs of Suspicious Activity

Red Flags in Messages

  • Urgent requests for personal information or money
  • Promises of prizes, rewards, or special offers that seem too good to be true
  • Requests to keep conversations secret from parents or authorities
  • Pressure to make quick decisions without time to think
  • Poor spelling, grammar, or formatting in professional communications
  • Mismatched email addresses or URLs (e.g., "paypa1.com" instead of "paypal.com")
  • Requests to download attachments or click suspicious links

Signs of Phishing

Legitimate Phishing
From: support@amazon.com From: support@amaz0n-security.com
Personalized greeting Generic "Dear Customer"
No urgency or threats "Act now or account will be closed!"
Secure company website link Suspicious or shortened URL

What to Do If You Suspect a Threat

  1. Don't click on links or download attachments
  2. Don't respond to suspicious messages
  3. Take a screenshot for evidence if needed
  4. Report the suspicious activity to the platform
  5. Tell a trusted adult (children and teens)
  6. Contact the company directly using official contact information (not from the suspicious message)

Family Safety Agreement

Create a family agreement that everyone signs and commits to following. This helps establish clear expectations and opens communication channels.

Our Family's Internet Safety Rules

  • We will keep all passwords private (except with parents)
  • We will ask permission before downloading apps or making purchases
  • We will be kind and respectful in all online interactions
  • We will think before we post or share content
  • We will never share personal information publicly
  • We will use devices in common family areas
  • We will respect screen time limits and tech-free times
  • We will tell a trusted adult if something makes us uncomfortable
  • We will not meet online friends in person without parent approval
  • We will regularly review and update our privacy settings together
