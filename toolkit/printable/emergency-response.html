🚨 IMMEDIATE DANGER - CALL 911

If there is an immediate threat to safety, call emergency services first, then follow the appropriate response below.

Account Hacked or Compromised

Immediate Actions (First 10 Minutes)

  1. Change Password Immediately (if you still have access)
    • Use a different device if possible
    • Create a strong, unique password
  2. Enable Two-Factor Authentication if not already active
  3. Check Recovery Information
    • Verify email and phone number are yours
    • Remove any unauthorized recovery methods
  4. Review Recent Activity
    • Check login history
    • Look for unauthorized posts or messages
    • Review connected apps and devices
  5. Alert Your Contacts
    • Warn friends that your account may send spam
    • Post a notice if possible

Next Steps (First Hour)

  • Log out of all devices and sessions
  • Remove suspicious apps or browser extensions
  • Run antivirus scan on your devices
  • Check linked accounts (email, payment methods)
  • Report hack to platform support
  • Document everything with screenshots
  • Change passwords on other accounts using same password

Identity Theft Detected

Immediate Actions

  1. Place Fraud Alert
    • Contact one credit bureau (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion)
    • They'll notify the others
    • Initial alert lasts 1 year, free
  2. Review Credit Reports
    • Get free reports from annualcreditreport.com
    • Look for unauthorized accounts or inquiries
    • Document suspicious activity
  3. Report to FTC
    • Visit IdentityTheft.gov
    • Create recovery plan
    • Get official identity theft report
  4. File Police Report
    • Bring FTC report and documentation
    • Get copy of police report
    • Use for disputing fraudulent charges
  5. Contact Financial Institutions
    • Close compromised accounts
    • Open new accounts with fraud alerts
    • Dispute unauthorized transactions

Long-term Protection

  • Consider credit freeze for all bureaus
  • Monitor credit reports regularly
  • Sign up for identity theft protection service
  • Review all financial statements monthly
  • Keep detailed records of all steps taken

Harmful Content Encountered

Types of Harmful Content

  • Child sexual abuse material (CSAM)
  • Extreme violence or gore
  • Self-harm or suicide content
  • Terrorist or extremist material
  • Non-consensual intimate images

Response Protocol

  1. Stop Viewing Immediately - Close window/app
  2. Do NOT Share or Screenshot - This can be illegal
  3. Report to Platform - Use reporting tools
  4. Report to Authorities
    • CSAM: NCMEC CyberTipline (cybertipline.org or 1-800-843-5678)
    • Other illegal content: FBI IC3 (ic3.gov) or local police
  5. Seek Support
    • Talk to trusted adult
    • Consider counseling if traumatized

⚠️ For Parents

If your child encounters disturbing content, stay calm. Don't blame them. Reassure them they did nothing wrong by telling you. Seek professional help if they show signs of trauma.

Online Threats or Harassment

Immediate Response

  1. Document Everything
    • Screenshot all messages with dates/times
    • Save URLs and usernames
    • Don't delete anything yet
  2. Do Not Respond - Engaging can escalate
  3. Block the Person - On all platforms
  4. Report to Platform - Use built-in reporting
  5. Tell Someone - Parent, teacher, or trusted adult

When to Contact Police

Contact local police immediately if threats include:

  • Physical violence or harm
  • Sexual assault or exploitation
  • Stalking behavior
  • Threats to others
  • Extortion or blackmail
  • Sharing of intimate images without consent

Additional Resources

  • Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741
  • National Suicide Prevention Lifeline: 988
  • RAINN (Sexual Assault): 1-800-656-4673
  • Cyberbullying Research Center: cyberbullying.org

Device Lost or Stolen

Immediate Actions

  1. Use Find My Device
    • iPhone/iPad: iCloud.com/find
    • Android: android.com/find
    • Windows: account.microsoft.com/devices
    • Mac: iCloud.com/find
  2. Lock Device Remotely - Set lock screen message with contact info
  3. Change Important Passwords (from another device)
    • Email
    • Banking/financial
    • Social media
    • Cloud storage
  4. Contact Carrier - Suspend service to prevent unauthorized use
  5. File Police Report (if stolen) - Get report number for insurance
  6. Contact Insurance - If you have device insurance or homeowner's policy
  7. Erase Device Remotely (last resort if recovery unlikely)

Prevention for Next Time

  • Enable Find My Device before loss occurs
  • Keep serial number recorded separately
  • Regular backups to cloud
  • Strong passcode/biometric lock
  • Don't store passwords in plain text
  • Consider device insurance

Scam or Phishing Attempt

If You Clicked a Suspicious Link

  1. Don't Panic - Quick action can prevent damage
  2. Disconnect from Internet - Stop potential malware download
  3. Run Antivirus Scan - Full system scan
  4. Change Passwords (from clean device)
    • Start with email and banking
    • Then other important accounts
  5. Monitor Accounts
    • Check for unauthorized transactions
    • Watch for suspicious activity
    • Review account access logs
  6. Report Phishing
    • Forward email to reportphishing@apwg.org
    • Report to FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov
    • Alert the impersonated company

If You Gave Financial Information

  • Contact bank/credit card company immediately
  • Freeze cards if necessary
  • Monitor accounts daily for fraudulent charges
  • Place fraud alert on credit reports
  • File report with FTC at IdentityTheft.gov

Emergency Preparedness

Before an Emergency

  • Enable two-factor authentication on all accounts
  • Use password manager with strong unique passwords
  • Enable Find My Device features
  • Regular backups of important data
  • Keep list of account recovery information
  • Know how to contact platform support
  • Save emergency contact numbers
  • Discuss digital emergencies with family
  • Know where to report different types of incidents

Create Your Emergency Plan

Fill out and keep accessible:

  • List of all online accounts and associated emails
  • Location of password manager master password (secure location)
  • Device serial numbers
  • Important contacts (family, IT support, authorities)
  • Recovery codes for important accounts
  • Insurance policy numbers
