### 2. Cyberbullying Prevention and Response

#### Key Points to Cover
- Definition and examples of cyberbullying
- Difference between conflict and bullying
- Impact on mental health
- Bystander responsibility
- Reporting procedures
- Supporting victims
- Consequences for bullies

#### Warning Signs
**Someone Being Cyberbullied May:**
- Withdraw from family and friends
- Seem anxious about phone/computer use
- Appear upset after being online
- Become secretive about online activities
- Experience changes in mood, behavior, sleep, or appetite
- Avoid school or social situations

#### Response Strategies

**If You're Being Cyberbullied:**
1. Don't respond or retaliate
2. Save evidence (screenshots with dates)
3. Block the bully
4. Report to the platform
5. Tell a trusted adult
6. Report to school if it involves classmates

**If You Witness Cyberbullying:**
1. Don't join in or share the content
2. Reach out privately to the victim with support
3. Report the content to the platform
4. Tell a trusted adult if serious
5. Model positive behavior online

**For Parents/Educators:**
1. Believe and support the child
2. Don't blame the victim
3. Document everything
4. Report to appropriate authorities
5. Seek professional help if needed
6. Work with school to address incident

#### Discussion Prompts
- What counts as cyberbullying vs. just being mean?
- Why do you think people cyberbully?
- What would you do if you saw someone being bullied online?
- How can we create a culture where cyberbullying isn't tolerated?

#### Activities
- Role-play scenarios and practice responses
- Create anti-cyberbullying campaign materials
- Write support messages to put in a "kindness bank"

### 4. Privacy and Data Protection

#### Key Points to Cover
- What is personal information
- How data is collected online
- Cookies and tracking
- Data breaches and consequences
- Strong passwords and password managers
- Two-factor authentication
- VPNs and encryption (age-appropriate)
- Digital footprint awareness

#### Types of Personal Information

**Never Share:**
- Full name with strangers
- Home address
- Phone number
- School name/location
- Birth date
- Social Security Number
- Financial information
- Real-time location
- Travel plans
- Photos with identifying information

**Think Carefully About:**
- Email address
- Approximate age or grade
- General location (city/state)
- Photos that don't show identifying info
- Interests and hobbies

#### Password Security

**Creating Strong Passwords:**
- At least 12 characters
- Mix of uppercase, lowercase, numbers, symbols
- No personal information
- No dictionary words
- Unique for each account
- Use a passphrase (e.g., "MyDogLikes2EatTacos!")

**Password Management:**
- Use a password manager
- Enable two-factor authentication
- Never share passwords (except with parents if child)
- Change passwords after breaches
- Use biometric locks when available

#### Discussion Prompts
- What could someone do if they had your personal information?
- How do companies use the data they collect about you?
- What's in your digital footprint?
- How can you tell if a website is secure?

#### Activities
- Digital footprint audit (search yourself online)
- Password strength checker
- Create a data privacy plan
- Review privacy policies together (simplified version)

### 5. Online Predators and Grooming

**Note: This is a sensitive topic. Approach with care and age-appropriate language.**

#### Key Points to Cover
- Who predators are (often not strangers)
- Grooming tactics and warning signs
- Trusted adult network
- Body safety and consent
- Reporting mechanisms
- Recovery and support

#### Grooming Tactics

**Common Strategies:**
- Building trust and friendship
- Offering gifts, compliments, or attention
- Isolating from friends and family
- Sharing secrets
- Gradually introducing sexual content
- Pressuring to share photos
- Threatening to share information
- Asking to meet in person or move to private platforms

#### Warning Signs

**Red Flags in Online Relationships:**
- Adult who wants to be your "special friend"
- Requests to keep relationship secret
- Asks for personal information
- Compliments appearance frequently
- Sends gifts or money
- Wants to video chat or meet in person
- Asks for photos
- Discusses sexual topics
- Makes you feel uncomfortable
- Gets angry if you don't respond quickly

#### Safe Online Relationship Rules

**For Youth:**
- Only connect with people you know in real life
- Tell a parent if someone makes you uncomfortable
- Never agree to meet an online friend alone
- Don't share photos you wouldn't show your family
- If something feels wrong, it probably is
- You won't get in trouble for telling the truth

**For Parents:**
- Know who your child talks to online
- Have ongoing conversations about online safety
- Create a non-judgmental environment for disclosure
- Monitor age-appropriately
- Know how to report to platforms and authorities

#### Discussion Prompts
- What makes a relationship healthy vs. unhealthy?
- How can you tell if someone online is who they say they are?
- What would you do if someone online made you uncomfortable?
- Why might someone be afraid to tell about an uncomfortable situation?

#### Resources for Reporting
- National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC): CyberTipline
- FBI IC3 (Internet Crime Complaint Center)
- Local law enforcement
- Platform reporting tools

### 6. Mental Health and Screen Time

#### Key Points to Cover
- Impact of social media on mental health
- Comparison culture and self-esteem
- FOMO (fear of missing out)
- Doom scrolling and news anxiety
- Sleep disruption from blue light
- Physical health impacts (posture, eyes, fitness)
- Recognizing problematic use
- Creating healthy digital habits

#### Signs of Problematic Screen Time
- Neglecting responsibilities (school, chores, relationships)
- Losing interest in offline activities
- Irritability when unable to access devices
- Lying about or hiding screen time
- Using devices to escape problems
- Sleep disruption
- Physical symptoms (headaches, eye strain, neck pain)

#### Creating Healthy Habits

**Individual Strategies:**
- Set specific time limits
- Use app timers and screen time tools
- Take regular breaks (20-20-20 rule for eyes)
- Put devices away during meals and before bed
- Find offline hobbies and activities
- Practice mindfulness about device use
- Curate your feed (unfollow accounts that make you feel bad)

**Family Strategies:**
- Screen-free zones and times
- Model healthy tech use
- Alternative activities together
- Open conversations about tech use
- Flexible boundaries that evolve with age
- Family media agreements

#### Discussion Prompts
- How does social media make you feel?
- What do you do when you're bored vs. what did people do before smartphones?
- How do you know if you're spending too much time on devices?
- What activities make you happier than screen time?

#### Activities
- Screen time audit (track usage for a week)
- Digital detox challenge
- Create a "boredom jar" with offline activity ideas
- Design a healthy daily routine with balanced tech use

## Presentation Skills and Tips

### Creating Engaging Presentations

#### Structure
1. **Attention-Grabbing Opener** (2-3 min)
   - Shocking statistic
   - Compelling story
   - Thought-provoking question
   - Brief video clip

2. **Set Expectations** (1-2 min)
   - Learning objectives
   - Agenda overview
   - Ground rules
   - Encourage questions

3. **Core Content** (40-60% of time)
   - Break into 3-5 main points
   - Mix presentation styles
   - Include visuals and examples
   - Check for understanding

4. **Interactive Elements** (20-30% of time)
   - Discussion questions
   - Small group activities
   - Polls or quizzes
   - Hands-on practice

5. **Q&A and Discussion** (10-20% of time)
   - Open floor for questions
   - Address concerns
   - Share additional resources

6. **Strong Closing** (3-5 min)
   - Recap key takeaways
   - Call to action
   - Share resources
   - Thank participants

#### Visual Design Tips
- Use simple, clean slides
- Limit text (6x6 rule: max 6 lines, 6 words each)
- Use high-quality images
- Consistent fonts and colors
- Include sources for statistics
- Make it accessible (readable fonts, good contrast)

#### Engagement Techniques
- Ask questions throughout
- Use real-life examples and stories
- Include humor (appropriately)
- Vary your delivery (pace, volume, energy)
- Move around the room
- Make eye contact
- Use participants' names
- Acknowledge contributions

### Handling Difficult Situations

**Disruptive Participants**
- Address privately if possible
- Redirect attention
- Use humor to defuse
- Set clear expectations
- Get support from organizer if needed

**Difficult Questions**
- Thank them for the question
- Be honest if you don't know
- Offer to follow up
- Ask if anyone else has insights
- Stay calm and composed

**Technical Difficulties**
- Have backup plans
- Stay calm and flexible
- Use the opportunity to engage audience
- Be prepared to present without slides

**Low Energy/Engagement**
- Change activities
- Take a stretch break
- Ask a provocative question
- Share an interesting story
- Do a quick interactive exercise
