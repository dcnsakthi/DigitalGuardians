## Understanding Digital Risks

### Common Online Threats
- **Cyberbullying**: Harassment through digital platforms
- **Online Predators**: Adults who build relationships with children for harmful purposes
- **Inappropriate Content**: Access to age-inappropriate material
- **Privacy Violations**: Sharing personal information that could be misused
- **Scams and Phishing**: Attempts to steal information or money
- **Gaming Addiction**: Excessive time spent gaming affecting daily life
- **Social Media Pressure**: Unrealistic expectations and mental health impacts

## Age-Appropriate Guidelines

### Ages 5-8: Early Learners
- **Screen Time**: 1 hour per day
- **Recommended Activities**: Educational games, supervised video watching
- **Key Rules**: 
  - Never share personal information
  - Always ask before downloading anything
  - Use devices in common areas only

### Ages 9-12: Growing Independence
- **Screen Time**: 1-2 hours per day
- **Recommended Activities**: Homework research, creative projects, approved social apps
- **Key Rules**:
  - Think before posting
  - Report uncomfortable situations
  - No private messaging with strangers
  - Check privacy settings together

### Ages 13-17: Teenagers
- **Screen Time**: 2-3 hours per day (recreational)
- **Recommended Activities**: Social networking, content creation, learning platforms
- **Key Rules**:
  - Understand digital footprint
  - Be critical of online information
  - Respect others online
  - Know how to block and report

## Setting Up Parental Controls

### Device-Level Controls

#### Windows
1. Go to Settings > Accounts > Family & other users
2. Add a family member
3. Set content restrictions and screen time limits

#### Mac
1. System Preferences > Screen Time
2. Enable parental controls
3. Set app limits and content filters

#### iOS
1. Settings > Screen Time
2. Enable Screen Time
3. Set Downtime and App Limits
4. Configure Content & Privacy Restrictions

#### Android
1. Settings > Digital Wellbeing & parental controls
2. Set up Family Link
3. Configure app restrictions and screen time

### Router-Level Controls
- Enable content filtering at the router level
- Set internet access schedules
- Monitor connected devices
- Consider using OpenDNS Family Shield

### App-Specific Controls
- YouTube: Enable Restricted Mode
- Netflix: Set up Kids profiles with age ratings
- Gaming Consoles: Use built-in parental controls
- Social Media: Enable privacy settings and reporting features

## Monitoring Without Invading Privacy

### Healthy Monitoring Practices
✅ **DO:**
- Have open conversations about online activities
- Explain why monitoring exists (safety, not punishment)
- Review privacy settings together
- Spot-check browsing history occasionally
- Follow your child's public social media accounts
- Use monitoring as a teaching opportunity

❌ **DON'T:**
- Read every private message
- Use spyware without your child's knowledge
- Punish immediately without understanding context
- Share their posts or embarrass them online
- Violate their trust without serious cause

### Building Trust
- Start with open dialogue
- Gradually increase privacy as they demonstrate responsibility
- Respect age-appropriate privacy needs
- Be transparent about your monitoring approach

## Recognizing Warning Signs

### Behavioral Changes
- Suddenly secretive about online activities
- Quickly switches screens when you approach
- Emotional distress after using devices
- Changes in sleep patterns or mood
- Withdrawal from family and friends
- Declining school performance

### Digital Red Flags
- Receiving gifts from unknown people
- Having multiple accounts or secret profiles
- Excessive time spent online
- Creating content that seems inappropriate
- New online friends they're reluctant to discuss

## Taking Action

### If You Discover Cyberbullying
1. Don't react immediately - gather evidence (screenshots)
2. Block the bully on all platforms
3. Report to the platform
4. Contact school if it involves classmates
5. Consider involving law enforcement for serious threats
6. Provide emotional support to your child

### If You Suspect a Predator
1. **DO NOT** confront the person online
2. Stop all communication immediately
3. Preserve all evidence (messages, profiles, screenshots)
4. Report to local law enforcement
5. Report to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) at CyberTipline.org
6. Report to the platform

### If Inappropriate Content Is Accessed
1. Stay calm and don't shame your child
2. Discuss why the content is inappropriate
3. Adjust parental controls and filters
4. Use it as a teaching moment
5. Monitor more closely temporarily

### Emergency Resources
- **National Suicide Prevention Lifeline**: 988 (US)
- **Crisis Text Line**: Text HOME to 741741
- **NCMEC CyberTipline**: CyberTipline.org or 1-800-843-5678
- **FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center**: IC3.gov
- **Cyberbullying Research Center**: cyberbullying.org
