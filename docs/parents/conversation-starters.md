# Conversation Starters for Digital Safety

Starting conversations about online safety with your children can feel awkward or challenging. These conversation starters are designed to help open dialogue naturally and create opportunities for meaningful discussions.

## General Principles

### Creating the Right Environment
- Choose a relaxed, private setting
- Avoid lecturing; aim for dialogue
- Ask open-ended questions
- Listen more than you talk
- Show genuine interest without judgment
- Share your own experiences

### Conversation Goals
- Build trust and open communication
- Understand your child's digital world
- Share values and expectations
- Problem-solve together
- Reinforce that you're there to help, not punish

## For Young Children (Ages 5-9)

### Getting Started
- "What's your favorite thing to do online?"
- "Can you show me the games/apps you like?"
- "Who do you like to talk to online?"
- "Have you seen anything online that made you feel uncomfortable?"

### Teaching Boundaries
- "Let's talk about what information is private, like our address. What else should we keep private?"
- "If someone online asks you where you live or what school you go to, what would you do?"
- "Who are the safe adults you can talk to if something online worries you?"

### Building Awareness
- "Sometimes people online might not be who they say they are. Can we talk about that?"
- "If you see something that makes you feel sad, scared, or confused online, what would you do?"
- "Let's make a list together of websites and apps that are okay to use."

### Making it Fun
- "Can you teach me how your favorite game works?"
- "What do you think makes someone a good digital citizen?"
- "If you were making rules for the internet, what would they be?"

## For Pre-Teens (Ages 10-12)

### Social Media & Communication
- "Many kids your age are starting to use social media. What do you think about it?"
- "How do you decide if something is okay to post or share?"
- "Have you ever seen someone being mean to another person online?"
- "What would you do if a friend shared something embarrassing about you?"

### Privacy & Personal Information
- "Let's look at your privacy settings together. Do you know what each one means?"
- "What do you think could happen if someone finds out too much about you online?"
- "Have you ever been asked for personal information online? How did you handle it?"

### Critical Thinking
- "How can you tell if something you read online is true?"
- "Have you ever seen ads trying to get you to buy or click something?"
- "What do you do when you're not sure if a website is safe?"

### Peer Relationships
- "How do you handle it when there's drama in group chats?"
- "Do you feel pressure to respond to messages right away?"
- "Have any of your friends had problems online?"

## For Teenagers (Ages 13-18)

### Digital Reputation
- "Have you thought about how your online presence might affect your future?"
- "What do you think future employers or colleges might see if they look you up?"
- "How do you decide what's appropriate to share online?"
- "Have you ever regretted posting something?"

### Relationships & Boundaries
- "How do you handle it when someone online makes you uncomfortable?"
- "What are your thoughts on online dating/relationships?"
- "How do you tell the difference between healthy and unhealthy online relationships?"
- "Have you or your friends experienced pressure to share photos or information you weren't comfortable with?"

### Mental Health & Wellbeing
- "How does social media make you feel? Energized? Anxious? Connected? Lonely?"
- "Do you ever feel like you're spending too much time online?"
- "How do you handle comparison or FOMO (Fear of Missing Out) online?"
- "What do you do when you need a break from social media?"

### Serious Topics
- "If someone was being cyberbullied, what would you advise them to do?"
- "Have you ever felt pressured to engage in risky behavior online?"
- "What would you do if someone sent you inappropriate content?"
- "Do you know how to report concerning behavior or content?"

### Independence & Responsibility
- "As you get older, I want to give you more privacy. How can we balance trust and safety?"
- "What are your thoughts on our current rules about technology?"
- "How would you like to earn more digital freedom?"
- "What worries you most about online safety?"

## Situation-Specific Conversations

### When Getting a New Device or App
- "Before we set this up, let's talk about how you'll use it safely."
- "What privacy settings should we look at together?"
- "What are the risks we should be aware of?"
- "Let's agree on how we'll check in about this."

### After Hearing About a Digital Safety Issue in the News
- "Did you hear about [incident]? What do you think about it?"
- "How do you think something like that could be prevented?"
- "Does anything like that happen in your online spaces?"

### When You Notice Warning Signs
- "I've noticed you seem [upset/secretive/anxious] when you're online. Is everything okay?"
- "You can talk to me about anything. I'm here to help, not judge."
- "If something's bothering you online, we can figure it out together."

### When They Come to You With a Problem
- "Thank you for telling me. That took courage."
- "You did the right thing by coming to me."
- "Let's work through this together. What do you think we should do?"
- "How are you feeling about this?"

## Conversation Techniques

### Active Listening
✅ "Tell me more about that."
✅ "How did that make you feel?"
✅ "What do you think about...?"
✅ "Help me understand..."

❌ "You should have..."
❌ "Why didn't you...?"
❌ "That was stupid."
❌ "I told you so."

### Asking Follow-Up Questions
- "What happened next?"
- "How did you handle that?"
- "What do your friends think?"
- "What would you do differently?"

### Sharing Without Lecturing
- "When I was your age, I didn't have social media, but I remember when..."
- "I've made mistakes online too. Let me tell you about..."
- "I'm still learning about this stuff. Can we figure it out together?"

### Problem-Solving Together
- "What do you think we should do about this?"
- "What would be a good rule that keeps you safe but gives you freedom?"
- "How can I support you with this?"

## Making It a Habit

### Regular Check-Ins
- Weekly: "What was the most interesting thing you saw online this week?"
- Monthly: "Are our rules still working for you? Should we adjust anything?"
- Quarterly: "Let's review your privacy settings together."

### Natural Opportunities
- During car rides
- While cooking together
- During family meals (tech-free)
- Before bed
- During tech time (ask them to show you)

### Planned Discussions
- Schedule "digital safety chats" monthly
- Make it a two-way conversation
- Keep it age-appropriate
- Follow up on previous conversations

## When Conversations Are Difficult

### If Your Child Won't Open Up
- Don't force it
- Try again at a different time
- Use indirect methods (movies, news, friend situations)
- Write a letter or text if face-to-face is too uncomfortable
- Consider involving another trusted adult

### If You Discover Something Concerning
- Stay calm
- Thank them for being honest (if they told you)
- Focus on problem-solving, not punishment
- Get professional help if needed
- Follow up regularly

### If You Don't Know the Answer
- "That's a great question. I don't know, but let's find out together."
- "I need to think about that. Can we talk about it tomorrow?"
- "Let's research that together."

## Sample Conversation Scenarios

### Scenario 1: First Social Media Account
**Parent:** "I see you're interested in [Instagram/TikTok]. Let's talk about it."
**Discuss:** Privacy settings, who to follow, what to share, handling strangers, time limits
**Outcome:** Create account together with agreed rules

### Scenario 2: Concerning Content
**Parent:** "I noticed some concerning content on your device. Can we talk about it?"
**Approach:** Non-judgmental, focused on understanding, educational opportunity
**Outcome:** Clear boundaries, monitoring plan, follow-up conversation

### Scenario 3: Cyberbullying Victim
**Parent:** "You seem upset. Is something going on online?"
**Approach:** Supportive, believing, action-oriented
**Outcome:** Report incident, document evidence, emotional support, school involvement if needed

### Scenario 4: Too Much Screen Time
**Parent:** "I'm concerned about the amount of time we're all spending on devices. Let's talk as a family."
**Approach:** Inclusive, family-wide, solution-focused
**Outcome:** Family agreement on tech-free times and spaces

## Resources for Extended Conversations

### Discussion Prompts
- Use current events as teaching moments
- Watch documentaries together about technology
- Read articles about digital trends
- Discuss fictional scenarios

### Activities to Do Together
- Review privacy settings as a team
- Create a family digital safety agreement
- Role-play difficult situations
- Research a new platform together

### When to Seek Help
If conversations reveal:
- Severe cyberbullying
- Contact with predators
- Exposure to inappropriate content
- Signs of addiction
- Mental health concerns
- Illegal activity

Contact: [Local Support Services](local-support.md) or [Professional Help](professional-help.md)

## Remember

- **Be Patient:** Building open communication takes time
- **Be Consistent:** Regular conversations work better than one big talk
- **Be Honest:** Admit when you don't know something
- **Be Supportive:** Your child should see you as a resource, not a threat
- **Be Flexible:** Adapt your approach as your child grows

---

**The goal isn't perfection; it's connection. Keep the lines of communication open, and your child will come to you when they need help.**
