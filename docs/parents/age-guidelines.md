# Age-Appropriate Digital Safety Guidelines

This guide helps parents understand what digital skills and safety knowledge are appropriate at different developmental stages, and what to expect from children at various ages.

## Overview by Age Group

| Age Range | Key Focus | Supervision Level | Typical Activities |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 0-2 years | Minimal exposure | Full supervision | Video calls with family |
| 3-5 years | Guided exploration | Full supervision | Educational apps, videos |
| 6-9 years | Basic safety concepts | Active supervision | Games, videos, basic browsing |
| 10-12 years | Digital citizenship | Regular check-ins | Social media, messaging, gaming |
| 13-15 years | Critical thinking | Periodic monitoring | Full social media, research, content creation |
| 16-18 years | Independence & responsibility | Trust with verification | Career preparation, financial literacy |

---

## Ages 0-2 Years: Minimal Digital Exposure

### Developmental Stage
- Rapid brain development
- Learning through physical exploration
- Developing language and social skills
- Building attachment relationships

### Recommended Screen Time
- **Video calls**: Encouraged for family connection
- **Other content**: Avoid or extremely limited
- **WHO/AAP Recommendation**: No screen time except video calls

### What They Can Learn
- Recognize faces on video calls
- Wave or interact during video chats
- Simple cause-and-effect (tap = response)

### Parental Role
✅ **Do:**
- Prioritize face-to-face interaction
- Use technology for family connection
- Model healthy device boundaries
- Provide rich real-world experiences

❌ **Don't:**
- Use devices as babysitters
- Allow independent device use
- Prioritize screens over interaction
- Expose to advertising or fast-paced content

### Red Flags
- Preference for screen over human interaction
- Delayed speech or social development
- Poor sleep patterns
- Lack of physical play

## Ages 3-5 Years: Guided Digital Exploration

### Developmental Stage
- Concrete thinking
- Learning basic rules
- Developing self-control
- Building curiosity about the world

### Recommended Screen Time
- **Maximum**: 1 hour per day
- **Type**: High-quality educational content
- **Method**: Co-viewing with parent

### What They Can Learn
- How to navigate simple apps
- Basic mouse/touchscreen skills
- Identify letters, numbers, shapes
- Simple problem-solving in educational games
- Concept that screens have an "off" button

### Age-Appropriate Activities
✅ **Suitable:**
- Educational apps (PBS Kids, Sesame Street)
- Age-appropriate videos
- Simple creative apps (drawing)
- Video calls with family
- Parent-guided browsing

❌ **Not Suitable:**
- Social media
- Multiplayer games with strangers
- Independent browsing
- YouTube without restrictions
- Fast-paced, violent content

### Safety Basics to Teach
- "Ask mommy/daddy before using tablet"
- "If you see something scary, close it and tell me"
- "We don't talk to people we don't know"
- Basic password concept (but parent enters it)

### Parental Role
✅ **Do:**
- Always supervise screen time
- Co-view and discuss content
- Set up devices in common areas
- Use parental controls and kid-specific apps
- Balance screen time with active play
- Create tech-free routines

### Tools for Parents
- YouTube Kids (supervised mode)
- Amazon FreeTime
- PBS Kids Video
- Kid-safe browsers
- Built-in parental controls

### Red Flags
- Tantrums when devices are removed
- Prefers screens to play with friends
- Nightmares from content
- Mimicking inappropriate behavior
- Decline in physical activity

## Ages 6-9 Years: Basic Safety Concepts

### Developmental Stage
- Concrete operational thinking
- Understanding rules and consequences
- Developing reading skills
- Building peer relationships
- Emerging independence

### Recommended Screen Time
- **Weekdays**: 1-2 hours (after homework)
- **Weekends**: 2-3 hours
- **Balance**: Mix educational and entertainment

### What They Can Learn
- Personal information should be private
- Not everyone online is trustworthy
- How to navigate websites safely
- Basic password safety
- Difference between real and pretend online
- How to ask for help
- Being kind online

### Age-Appropriate Activities
✅ **Suitable:**
- Educational websites (National Geographic Kids)
- Age-appropriate games (Minecraft, Roblox with supervision)
- Email (supervised)
- Simple research for homework
- Creative projects (coding, digital art)
- Video calls
- Age-appropriate YouTube channels (supervised)

⚠️ **With Supervision:**
- Social media (not recommended until 13+)
- Multiplayer games
- Messaging apps
- Online communities

❌ **Not Suitable:**
- Unsupervised social media
- Adult-oriented content
- Meeting online friends in person
- Sharing personal information

### Safety Skills to Teach

**Personal Information:**
- Full name, address, phone number = private
- School name and location = private
- Parents' information = private
- Photos showing home/school = private
- Passwords = secret (except from parents)

**Online Interactions:**
- Only talk to people you know in real life
- Tell parent if someone asks personal questions
- Don't accept friend requests from strangers
- It's okay to say no to uncomfortable requests
- Not everyone is who they say they are

**Content Safety:**
- If something makes you uncomfortable, close it
- Not everything online is true
- Some videos/games are for older kids
- Ask before downloading anything
- Don't click on pop-ups or ads

**Digital Citizenship:**
- Be kind online like in person
- Don't say mean things
- Tell an adult if someone is being mean
- Ask permission before sharing others' photos

### Parental Role
✅ **Do:**
- Know passwords to all accounts
- Regularly check browsing history
- Use parental controls and filters
- Set up devices in common areas
- Have regular conversations about online activities
- Establish clear rules and consequences
- Friend/follow children on platforms they use
- Teach by example

### Tools for Parents
- Parental control software (Qustodio, Net Nanny)
- Kid-safe search engines (Kiddle, KidRex)
- Gaming platform parental controls
- Browser history monitoring
- Time limit apps
- YouTube restricted mode

### Red Flags
- Secretive behavior about device use
- Quickly switching screens when you approach
- Spending excessive time online
- New "online friends" they mention
- Decline in school performance
- Changes in mood after device use
- Wanting device in bedroom
- Insisting on privacy

## Ages 10-12 Years: Pre-Teen Digital Citizenship

### Developmental Stage
- Abstract thinking emerges
- Intense peer relationships
- Seeking independence
- Self-consciousness increases
- Testing boundaries

### Recommended Screen Time
- **Weekdays**: 2-3 hours (after responsibilities)
- **Weekends**: 3-4 hours
- **Flexibility**: Based on demonstrated responsibility

### What They Can Learn
- Digital footprint concept
- Privacy settings and why they matter
- Cyberbullying awareness
- Critical evaluation of online content
- Consequences of online actions
- How to report problems
- Balancing online and offline life
- Digital reputation basics

### Age-Appropriate Activities
✅ **Suitable:**
- Social media (Instagram, TikTok) with heavy supervision
- Group messaging apps
- Online gaming with friends
- YouTube content creation (supervised)
- Educational platforms (Khan Academy)
- Research for school projects
- Creative coding/design
- Email communication

⚠️ **Needs Clear Rules:**
- Social media usage and posting
- Gaming with strangers
- Content creation/sharing
- Online shopping (with permission)
- Video sharing platforms

❌ **Still Not Appropriate:**
- Meeting online friends alone
- Adult-oriented platforms
- Unsupervised browsing
- Full privacy from parents
- Unmonitored social media

### Safety Skills to Teach

**Social Media Literacy:**
- How to set account to private
- Who to accept as followers/friends
- What not to post (personal info, location, inappropriate content)
- Digital footprint is permanent
- Think before posting
- How to block and report
- Recognition of fake accounts

**Cyberbullying:**
- What constitutes cyberbullying
- Don't participate or forward
- How to be an upstander
- Document and report
- Tell trusted adult immediately
- It's not your fault if you're targeted

**Critical Thinking:**
- Not everything online is true
- How to fact-check information
- Recognizing ads and sponsored content
- Understanding persuasion techniques
- Identifying biased sources
- Questioning what you see

**Privacy & Security:**
- Strong password creation
- Never share passwords (except parents)
- Two-factor authentication
- Recognizing phishing attempts
- Privacy settings on all platforms
- Location sharing dangers

### Parental Role
✅ **Do:**
- Have regular, open conversations
- Know what platforms they use
- Check accounts regularly (with their knowledge)
- Teach rather than just restrict
- Respect growing need for privacy while maintaining safety
- Discuss real-world scenarios
- Model good digital behavior
- Create family media agreement together
- Gradually increase freedom with responsibility

### Tools for Parents
- Family Link (Google)
- Screen Time (Apple)
- Circle Home Plus
- Platform-specific parental controls
- Bark (monitoring app)
- OurPact

### Warning Signs & Red Flags
🚩 Sudden withdrawal from family
🚩 Secretive online behavior
🚩 Emotional responses to messages
🚩 Changes in friend groups
🚩 Decline in grades or activities
🚩 Sleep problems
🚩 Anxiety or depression
🚩 Defensive about device use
🚩 Multiple accounts or secret accounts
🚩 Receiving gifts from unknown sources

### When to Seek Help
- Evidence of cyberbullying
- Contact with predators
- Exposure to inappropriate content
- Behavioral changes
- Mental health concerns
- Self-harm or suicidal thoughts

## Ages 13-15 Years: Early Teen Independence

### Developmental Stage
- Identity formation
- Peer influence peaks
- Risk-taking behavior
- Emotional intensity
- Abstract reasoning develops
- Need for autonomy

### Recommended Screen Time
- **Collaborative limits**: Set together with teen
- **Focus**: Quality over quantity
- **Balance**: With sleep, homework, activities

### What They Can Learn
- Advanced privacy management
- Digital reputation impact on future
- Recognizing manipulation online
- Healthy online relationships
- Media literacy
- Digital wellness and screen time balance
- Legal implications of online behavior
- Protecting others' privacy

### Age-Appropriate Activities
✅ **Suitable:**
- Full social media use with guidelines
- Content creation (YouTube, TikTok)
- Online learning and research
- Gaming communities
- Creative pursuits (coding, design, music)
- Professional network building (LinkedIn for education)
- Blogging or vlogging
- Online activism (appropriate)

⚠️ **Discuss Guidelines:**
- Dating apps (generally not before 16-18)
- Live streaming
- Sharing personal content
- Online purchases
- Meeting online friends

### Safety Skills to Teach

**Digital Reputation:**
- How colleges and employers check online presence
- Cleaning up old posts
- Professional vs. personal accounts
- Long-term thinking about posts
- Google yourself regularly

**Advanced Privacy:**
- Metadata in photos
- Third-party app permissions
- Data collection and usage
- VPNs and encrypted messaging
- Digital rights and laws (PDPA in Singapore)

**Healthy Relationships Online:**
- Red flags in online relationships
- Sexting dangers and legal issues
- Pressure and coercion
- Setting boundaries
- When to end relationships
- Reporting mechanisms

**Financial Literacy:**
- Recognizing scams
- Safe online shopping
- Protecting payment information
- Understanding in-app purchases
- Cryptocurrency awareness

### Parental Role
✅ **Do:**
- Shift from monitoring to mentoring
- Have conversations, not interrogations
- Respect privacy while maintaining safety
- Discuss news stories about online issues
- Be available for questions
- Share your own experiences
- Trust but verify
- Focus on values, not just rules
- Recognize their growing maturity

❌ **Don't:**
- Snoop without reason
- Share their information on your social media
- Embarrass them online
- Use devices as only punishment method
- Ignore red flags
- Assume they know everything

### Tools for Parents
- Open communication (most important tool!)
- Spot-checks with their knowledge
- Social media literacy courses
- Family discussions
- Monitoring based on trust level
- Location sharing for safety (not surveillance)

### Red Flags (Serious Concerns)
🚨 Extreme secrecy about online activities
🚨 Multiple hidden accounts
🚨 Receiving gifts from online friends
🚨 Planning to meet online friends
🚨 Sexting or sharing explicit content
🚨 Evidence of online predation
🚨 Extreme mood changes related to device use
🚨 Self-harm content or communities
🚨 Eating disorder communities
🚨 Drug/substance-related content
🚨 Illegal activities
🚨 Being coerced or blackmailed

### Crisis Response
**If you discover serious issues:**
1. Stay calm
2. Don't destroy evidence
3. Document everything
4. Contact authorities if needed (Police: 999)
5. Seek professional help
6. Contact school if appropriate
7. Follow up with continuous support

**Resources:**
- Institute of Mental Health: 6389-2222
- TOUCH Cyber Wellness: 1800-377-2252
- Police: 999 or 1800-255-0000
- School counselor

## Ages 16-18 Years: Preparing for Adult Independence

### Developmental Stage
- Identity consolidation
- Future planning
- Increased autonomy
- Long-term thinking
- Adult responsibilities emerging

### Recommended Screen Time
- **Self-regulated** with guidance
- Focus on healthy balance
- Preparation for adult self-management

### What They Can Learn
- Complete digital literacy
- Professional online presence
- Advanced security practices
- Ethical technology use
- Digital wellness self-management
- Career implications of digital presence
- Financial cybersecurity
- Being a digital mentor to younger siblings/peers

### Age-Appropriate Activities
✅ **Suitable:**
- All social media platforms
- Professional networking (LinkedIn)
- Online job applications
- College research and applications
- Content creation as potential career
- Online banking (with guidance)
- Investment apps (with education)
- All appropriate online activities

⚠️ **Discuss Maturely:**
- Online dating (safety first)
- Sharing political views
- Digital activism
- Building personal brand
- Managing digital legacy
- Privacy vs. opportunity

### Safety Skills to Teach

**Professional Digital Presence:**
- Building LinkedIn profile
- Professional email etiquette
- Online portfolio creation
- Networking appropriately
- Separating personal and professional
- Employer perspective awareness

**Advanced Security:**
- Protecting financial information
- Recognizing advanced scams
- Secure communication for sensitive topics
- Data breach response
- Identity theft protection
- Password managers

**Legal Awareness:**
- Age of consent for various platforms
- Copyright and intellectual property
- Consequences of illegal online activity
- Cyberbullying laws
- Defamation and harassment laws
- Digital evidence in legal proceedings

**Preparation for Independence:**
- Managing own digital life
- Making informed decisions
- Recognizing when to seek help
- Taking responsibility for actions
- Mentoring younger teens/siblings

### Parental Role
✅ **Do:**
- Transition to consultant role
- Provide guidance when asked
- Trust their judgment (with basis)
- Discuss complex ethical situations
- Help with professional development
- Be available for support
- Share adult experiences
- Prepare them for post-high school digital life

### Gradual Independence
As they demonstrate responsibility:
- Reduce active monitoring
- Increase privacy
- Allow more autonomy
- Trust their decisions
- Shift to peer-like discussions
- Prepare for full independence

### Red Flags at This Age
🚩 Gambling problems
🚩 Excessive debt from online shopping
🚩 Involvement in illegal activities
🚩 Toxic relationships
🚩 Addiction to gaming/social media
🚩 Mental health deterioration
🚩 Compromising future opportunities

### Preparing for Adulthood
**Before age 18, discuss:**
- Managing all own accounts
- Financial cybersecurity
- Professional reputation
- Legal responsibilities as adult
- Where to get help as adult
- Ongoing digital literacy

## General Principles Across All Ages

### Progressive Trust Model
- **Start with restrictions**
- **Earn freedom through responsibility**
- **Increase autonomy gradually**
- **Adjust based on maturity**
- **Recognize individual differences**

### Communication Over Control
- Open dialogue > surveillance
- Teaching > restricting
- Understanding > punishing
- Guidance > rules alone

### Family Consistency
- Apply rules fairly to all children
- Adjust for age differences
- Parents model behavior
- Regular family discussions
- Update agreements as children grow

### Signs of Healthy Digital Life (All Ages)
✅ Balance with offline activities
✅ Maintains real-world friendships
✅ Good sleep habits
✅ Open communication
✅ Responsible behavior
✅ Academic success
✅ Physical activity
✅ Diverse interests

### Universal Red Flags (Any Age)
🚨 Secretive behavior
🚨 Dramatic personality changes
🚨 Isolation from family
🚨 Declining performance (school/work)
🚨 Sleep problems
🚨 Physical symptoms
🚨 Mentions of self-harm
🚨 Evidence of exploitation

## Remember

**Every Child is Different**
- These are guidelines, not rigid rules
- Adapt based on your child's maturity
- Consider individual circumstances
- Be flexible while maintaining safety

**You Know Your Child Best**
- Trust your instincts
- Seek help when needed
- Stay involved
- Keep learning together

**Digital Safety is a Journey**
- Not a one-time conversation
- Evolves as technology changes
- Requires ongoing attention
- Gets easier with practice
