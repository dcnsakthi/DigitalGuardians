# Lesson Plan: Think Before You Post

## Lesson Overview

**Title:** Think Before You Post: Understanding Digital Footprint  
**Grade Level:** Primary 4-6 (Ages 10-12)  
**Duration:** 45 minutes  
**Subject Area:** Character & Citizenship Education / Digital Literacy  
**Learning Mode:** Interactive, Discussion-Based

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. Define "digital footprint" and explain why it matters
2. Identify types of information that should not be shared online
3. Apply the T.H.I.N.K. framework before posting content
4. Evaluate sample social media posts for appropriateness
5. Demonstrate understanding of long-term consequences of online actions

## Key Concepts

- **Digital Footprint**: The trail of data you leave behind when using the internet
- **Permanence**: Online content can last forever, even if deleted
- **Privacy**: Controlling what information others can see about you
- **Reputation**: How others perceive you based on your digital footprint

## Lesson Outline

### Introduction (5 minutes)

**Hook Activity: "Digital Detective"**

1. Display a fictional social media profile on screen (carefully curated example)
2. Ask students: "What can you learn about this person from their posts?"
3. Have students call out observations (age, location, school, hobbies, family, etc.)
4. Discussion question: "Were you surprised by how much we could learn?"

**Transition Statement:**
"Today we'll learn why we need to be careful about what we share online and how to make smart decisions before posting."

**Learning Objectives:**
Briefly share what students will learn today (use student-friendly language)

### Main Content (20 minutes)

**Part 1: What is a Digital Footprint? (7 minutes)**

**Explanation:**
- Define digital footprint in simple terms
- Use footprint analogy: "Just like you leave footprints when you walk on sand, you leave digital footprints when you go online"
- Explain two types:
  - **Passive footprint**: Data collected about you (websites you visit, searches)
  - **Active footprint**: What you deliberately share (posts, comments, photos)

**Video:** Show "Your Digital Footprint" video (3 minutes)

**Quick Discussion:**
- "What surprised you about the video?"
- "Can you give examples of your digital footprint?"
- "Who can see your digital footprint?"

**Key Points to Emphasize:**
- Digital footprints can last forever
- Once posted, you lose control of content
- Future schools, employers, others can see what you post
- Even "deleted" content can be saved by screenshots

**Part 2: The T.H.I.N.K. Framework (8 minutes)**

**Introduce T.H.I.N.K.:**
Display poster and explain each letter:

- **T**rue: Is what I'm sharing accurate and factual?
- **H**elpful: Does this add value or help someone?
- **I**nspiring: Is this uplifting and positive?
- **N**ecessary: Does this need to be shared?
- **K**ind: Is this respectful and not hurtful to anyone?

**Interactive Example:**
Show a sample post and model thinking through T.H.I.N.K. framework together

Example post: "Can't believe how boring today's assembly was! 😴"

- **T**rue: Maybe, but is it respectful?
- **H**elpful: No, doesn't help anyone
- **I**nspiring: No, it's negative
- **N**ecessary: No, doesn't need to be shared
- **K**ind: No, disrespectful to speakers

**Verdict**: Should NOT be posted

**Practice:**
Show 2-3 more examples quickly, have students use thumbs up/down to indicate post/don't post

**Part 3: What NOT to Share (5 minutes)**

**Brainstorm Activity:**
"What information should we NEVER share online?"

Create a list on whiteboard as students call out ideas:
- Full name, address, phone number
- School name and location
- Parents' information
- Passwords
- Photos showing house number/car license plate
- Current location (in real-time)
- Travel plans
- Birthday and age
- Financial information

**Visual Aid:**
Show "Red Light" list of never-share information

**Real-World Connection:**
"Why does this matter? Someone could use this information to find you, pretend to be you, or harm you or your family."

### Interactive Activity (15 minutes)

**Activity: "Should I Post This?" Scenarios**

**Setup:**
1. Divide class into groups of 4-5 students
2. Give each group:
   - Set of scenario cards
   - Chart paper
   - Markers

**Instructions:**
1. Each group reviews 4-5 scenarios
2. For each scenario, discuss:
   - Should this be posted? Why or why not?
   - Apply T.H.I.N.K. framework
   - What could be the consequences?
3. Record decisions on chart paper
4. Prepare to share one scenario with class

**Sample Scenarios (Provided on Cards):**

**Scenario 1:**
Maya wants to post a photo from her birthday party showing her new bike, with her house number visible in the background.
- **Discussion**: House number = personal info, safety risk

**Scenario 2:**
Raj got an A+ on his math test and wants to post a photo of his test paper showing his full name and school.
- **Discussion**: Personal info visible, okay to share achievement differently

**Scenario 3:**
Sara wants to post "Great day at Sentosa Beach!" with a selfie.
- **Discussion**: Generally okay, but should wait until home (not real-time location)

**Scenario 4:**
Tom sees his friend posted something embarrassing and wants to share it with a funny caption.
- **Discussion**: Not kind, violates friend's privacy, could be bullying

**Scenario 5:**
Priya created digital artwork and wants to share it online.
- **Discussion**: Positive, creative, no personal info—good to share!

**Group Work Time:**
- Teacher circulates to facilitate discussions
- Ask probing questions: "What could happen if...?"
- Encourage students to consider multiple perspectives

**Gallery Walk (5 minutes):**
- Groups post their chart paper
- Students do quick gallery walk to see other groups' work
- Teacher highlights interesting points or common themes

### Class Discussion & Sharing (3 minutes)

**Debrief Questions:**
- "What was the hardest scenario to decide on? Why?"
- "What did you learn from other groups?"
- "How will this change what you post?"

**Share Out:**
Have 2-3 groups briefly share one interesting scenario they discussed

### Conclusion (2 minutes)

**Key Takeaways:**
Review the main points (use quick recap):
1. "Your digital footprint lasts forever"
2. "Use T.H.I.N.K. before posting"
3. "Protect personal information"
4. "Consider consequences before sharing"

**Real-World Application:**
"This week, before you post anything online, use T.H.I.N.K. to decide if you should share it."

**Preview Next Lesson:**
"Next time we'll talk about what to do if someone posts something mean about you or your friends."

## Assessment

### Formative Assessment (During Lesson)
- Observe participation in discussions
- Monitor group work conversations
- Check for understanding through thumbs up/down activity
- Ask probing questions during group work

### Summative Assessment (End of Lesson)
**Exit Ticket:** Students complete one of these:

**Option 1:** Short Written Response
"Explain in 3-5 sentences what you learned about digital footprints and what you will do differently."

**Option 2:** Scenario Evaluation
Provide one new scenario, students write whether they would post it and why/why not using T.H.I.N.K.

**Option 3:** Visual Summary
Draw and label the T.H.I.N.K. framework with one example

### Follow-Up Assessment (Optional)
- Have students journal about their online posts for one week
- Create a poster about digital footprint to display in class
- Write a letter to younger students about online safety

## Differentiation

### For Advanced Learners
- Discuss more complex scenarios (privacy settings, tagging others, etc.)
- Research real-world examples of digital footprint impacting careers
- Create public service announcement about digital footprint
- Explore legal aspects of online posting
- Design their own scenarios for peers

### For Struggling Learners
- Provide T.H.I.N.K. checklist to use during activity
- Pair with supportive partner
- Use more visual examples
- Simplify scenarios
- Provide sentence starters for discussions
- Allow verbal responses instead of written

### For English Language Learners
- Pre-teach vocabulary (footprint, permanent, privacy, reputation)
- Provide visual aids for all concepts
- Allow discussion in home language, then translate
- Provide scenario cards in multiple languages if available
- Partner with bilingual student
- Use more visual examples, less text

### For Special Needs Students
- Provide modified worksheet with pictures
- Allow use of assistive technology
- Smaller group size or one-on-one support
- Extended time for activities
- Concrete, personal examples
- Additional visual supports

## Extension Activities

### For Homework
1. **Family Discussion**: Share T.H.I.N.K. framework with family, discuss family rules about posting
2. **Digital Footprint Audit**: With parent, review own social media and identify anything to remove
3. **Interview Activity**: Ask an adult about something they're glad they didn't post online

### For Further Learning
1. **Create Content**: Design a T.H.I.N.K. poster or infographic
2. **Peer Education**: Present to younger class about digital footprints
3. **Research Project**: Investigate how colleges/employers check social media
4. **Creative Writing**: Write from the perspective of a social media post that regrets being posted

### Cross-Curricular Connections
- **English**: Writing unit on audience and purpose
- **Art**: Create digital footprint awareness campaign
- **ICT**: Learn about privacy settings on various platforms
- **Math**: Graph survey results about posting habits
- **Social Studies**: Compare digital citizenship to regular citizenship

## Common Misconceptions

**Misconception 1:** "If I delete it, it's gone forever."
- **Correction**: Explain screenshots, archives, backups

**Misconception 2:** "Only my friends can see what I post."
- **Correction**: Discuss privacy settings, shares, screenshots

**Misconception 3:** "What I post now doesn't matter because I'm just a kid."
- **Correction**: Share age-appropriate examples of lasting impact

**Misconception 4:** "I can make my account private and post whatever I want."
- **Correction**: Discuss that friends can share, privacy isn't foolproof

## Sensitive Topics

### If Students Bring Up:
- **Past inappropriate posts**: Handle privately, focus on learning and fixing
- **Cyberbullying experiences**: Provide support, follow school protocol
- **Parental monitoring**: Validate feelings but emphasize safety
- **Peer pressure to post**: Discuss strategies to resist

### Mandatory Reporting
Be aware of situations requiring reporting to school counselor/administration:
- Evidence of cyberbullying
- Exposure to inappropriate content
- Contact with predators
- Self-harm content
- Safety concerns

## Notes for Teachers

### Classroom Management Tips
- Keep energy high during video and activities
- Use timer for group work
- Have extra scenarios ready for fast finishers
- Bring discussions back to topic if they drift
- Validate all student contributions

### Making It Engaging
- Use current, relatable examples
- Include humor where appropriate
- Connect to students' real experiences
- Make it interactive, not just lecture
- Use variety of activities

### Safety Considerations
- Don't show real student social media
- Don't single out students who've made mistakes
- Create safe space for questions
- Emphasize learning, not shaming
- Follow up privately with concerned students
